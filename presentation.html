05 / Final insights · The investigation report

Three findings worth remembering.

When we stop looking at counts alone, a different picture of reported aircraft damage appears.

FINDING 01 / FREQUENCY

The most reported is not the most damaging.

56.7×
Higher observed damage rate for White-tailed deer than Mourning dove.
73.76% of 202 reports vs 1.30% of 6,693 reports.
FINDING 02 / ANIMAL SIZE

Larger animals stand out.

24.36%
Recorded damage rate for Large animals, compared with 1.49% for Small.
Large: 1,248 / 5,124 · Small: 1,090 / 73,307 reports.
FINDING 03 / CIRCUMSTANCES

Context changes the outcome.

~2×
Multiple-animal strikes showed almost double the recorded damage rate of single-animal strikes.
7.20% Multiple vs 3.58% Single · observational association.
DATA ≠ CAUSATION   •   These are rates among reported strikes. They are not the probability of a collision per flight.
06 / Conclusion · Final approach
The answer to our question
COUNT THE STRIKES.
THEN MEASURE THE DAMAGE.

Frequency alone hides important differences. Species, size, and the circumstances of a reported wildlife strike are associated with strikingly different damage outcomes.
